02 · Where to study

A small, reliable source stack for every topic

Use the topic-level links in the Phase I and Phase II maps below. The source shelf here tells you what each source is good for—and keeps the plan deliberately small.

The 2 + 1 rule
  1. Learn the concept: one static foundation or the exact syllabus scope; do not collect multiple full courses.
  2. Update and verify: use the responsible official institution for current rules, figures, schemes and report editions.
  3. Practise: use the supplied memory-based papers, one practice book or one mock series, then maintain an error log. The papers are useful practice, not official RBI papers.

For GA, make the official release the fact source; use one newspaper/explainer only to understand context. A monthly compilation can be an index, not a second authority.

Minimal source stack by need
  • Phase I GA + Phase II ESI/FM: RBI, PIB/myScheme, Budget/Economic Survey, MoSPI and a focused report list.
  • Static ESI: selected NCERT economics chapters; update arguments and figures from Survey/Budget/MoSPI.
  • Finance: RBI and SEBI/NISM for institutions, markets, regulation and risk; skip certification-level detail.
  • Management: official syllabus plus selective IGNOU eGyanKosh material; one standard text only if a concept remains unclear.
  • Phase I aptitude/English: one practice reference per weak subject, supplied PYPs and timed mocks. No paid course is required by this guide.

03 · Phase I score design

Protect the 60% floor in every section

Reference ranges below match the 200-number layout used in the searchable supplied compilations, not a guarantee of the 2027 official structure.

70% overall arithmetic: if the 2027 Phase I remains 80 GA + 30 Quant + 60 Reasoning + 30 English = 200 equally weighted one-mark items, 140 net marks is a 70% goal. A balanced practice profile near 56/80, 21/30, 42/60 and 21/30 is 140 raw correct only if unanswered/wrong responses incur no penalty. With negative marking, score to the official net formula and keep an accuracy buffer; confirm the notification's marks and penalty.
Guardrails against over-attempting
  • Track net marks, accuracy and time for each section separately; raw attempts can hide a negative-marking problem.
  • For GA, answer known facts and strong eliminations; do not turn uncertainty into blind guesses.
  • For Quant and Reasoning, set a time ceiling per item/set. Leave a time sink and collect easier marks elsewhere.
  • For English, passage evidence and grammar rules beat intuition. Maintain an error log.
  • Use two consecutive full mocks to decide whether the weakest section needs more time; don't redistribute study time based on one bad test.

What the paper actually tests
  • Identity: institution, ministry, regulator, report publisher, scheme or programme.
  • Precise attributes: objective, beneficiary, eligibility, component, limit/threshold, date, place, partner, host or chair.
  • Data literacy: rank/score, source, period, unit, top performer, measured dimension and change from the previous release.
  • Policy facts: target, budget/funding, extension, implementation channel, latest rule/status and intended outcome.
  • Phase II bridge: explain why the fact matters—growth, inflation, employment, inclusion, fiscal space, stability, climate or external-sector transmission.
GA study method that maximises recall
  1. Collect selectively: use primary releases first; retain a story only if it maps to RBI/banking, economy, government policy, reports, international affairs, social development, climate or major science/technology.
  2. Compress: make one card per event/report/scheme, with a 20–40 word fact core and one “why it matters” line.
  3. Retrieve: hide the notes; recall issuer, purpose, date, beneficiary/metric, one number and syllabus link. Revisit after 1, 3, 7, 21 days.
  4. Interleave: mix static banking/economy concepts with current-affairs cards; do a timed GA set and audit misses by fact type.
  5. Update: replace stale forecasts/amounts with the latest official release; keep publication vintage and reference period visible.

Schemes: learn the decision-relevant facts, not a catalogue

Scheme-related paper items repeatedly reduce to a small set of factual dimensions. The examples below are names/themes seen in the supplied compilations, not a frequency ranking.

How to answer scheme questions

Build a 7-field card: full name/acronym · ministry/implementer · objective · beneficiary and exclusions · benefit/component · funding/target/duration · latest change/status. Add one cross-link to jobs, agriculture, health, education, women, MSMEs, exports, energy, housing or financial inclusion.

High-return contrast: central-sector vs centrally sponsored; grant/subsidy vs credit/guarantee; announced vs operational; original vs extended/restructured; target vs achievement.

What not to do with schemes
  • Do not memorise a long scheme list without beneficiary, ministry and delivery mechanism.
  • Do not use stale allocations/eligibility; amounts and extension dates change yearly.
  • Do not overlearn state-level micro-schemes absent major national relevance.
  • Do not treat every budget line, law, campaign or public-sector programme as a scheme; confirm official design and current status.

Reports and indices worth the reading time

Prioritise the official executive summary, headline tables and India/state comparisons—not the entire document.

Report recall template: issuer → report/edition → what it measures → India/state score or rank → top performer → major change → one policy implication. The PYP trend is strongest for publisher/rank/metric/dimension-style recall, not full-report detail.

08 · Minimal, high-return operating plan

Study a small set deeply; keep the rest warm

Use percentages of your available study time so the routine scales to your schedule.

Weekly allocation
30%Phase II static core: ESI + FM + management
25%Phase I Quant + Reasoning timed practice
25%GA: current affairs, RBI, reports and schemes
10%English: daily reading, grammar and vocab
10%Mocks, descriptive writing and error review

This deliberately gives GA a dedicated quarter because its breadth feeds both phases. After two mocks, move up to 10 percentage points toward the weakest section only if its net score is below target; preserve GA recall and Phase II writing even while Phase I speed work intensifies.

One repeatable study loop
  1. Learn: one syllabus subtopic from a primary source and concise notes.
  2. Retrieve: close the notes and explain the concept, mechanism and current example from memory.
  3. Apply: timed practice or a short analytical paragraph.
  4. Correct: record error cause, correct rule and the next review date.
  5. Space: revisit after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days; drop material only after repeated accurate retrieval.
Phase II answer standard
  • Open with a direct thesis/definition; structure with short headings and linked arguments.
  • Use one or two precise, dated indicators; explain the mechanism and limitation rather than stacking statistics.
  • Balance growth with inclusion, stability, fiscal capacity, rights or sustainability where relevant.
  • End with feasible policy measures and a concise conclusion.
  • For FM, add regulator/institution, instrument, risk and control; for Management, name the theory only when it explains the scenario.
Current-affairs workflow (minimal)
  • Use a weekly primary-source sweep: RBI, Ministry of Finance/PIB, Economic Survey/Budget, IMF/World Bank, Commerce, IMD and relevant regulator releases.
  • Keep one page per theme—not one page per headline: event, date, issuer, mechanism, one figure, syllabus link, likely analytical angle.
  • Review monthly and again in the final 12–15 months before the exam; correct old forecasts with the newest vintage.
  • Do not memorise a news explainer's numbers until confirmed in the original release. The links below identify leads and primary verification routes.
